Bringing transfers, Pix keys and refund tracking to Mercado Pago’s desktop web, for people who manage their account from a computer.

Summary

On desktop, Mercado Pago users had a Pix experience that depended on the app, which hurt sellers who ran their business from a computer. In this project, I designed the Pix area for the web, with Pix key registration and management, transfers and refund-request tracking.

I also adapted the Pix key registration and portability flows for the web, which depended on this area to exist, and illustrated the first-time use (FTU) screens.

Context & challenge

A study by our research team on Pix showed that many users confused basic concepts, didn’t understand how key portability worked and couldn’t remember which keys they had registered. We also saw complaints that the experience was inconsistent with the app and that people couldn’t do everything in one place.

The same page had to make sense for someone with no Pix key, someone moving a key from another bank (portability) and someone waiting on a refund request, for both individuals and sellers. On top of that, some components had been designed for mobile, so we had to rethink how they would behave on the web.

Process

01

Internal benchmark

Used the mobile experience as a reference and decided what to keep, adapt or drop, preserving as much of the original experience as possible within the technical limits of the web.

02

Map the paths

Mapped the flows for individuals and sellers, considering the different error and success states, before drawing screens.

03

Document every state

Documented every key use case (no key, with key, portability in and out, multiple portabilities) and the MedPix display rules.

04

Review before handoff

Feedback rounds with the team, design system specialists, product and engineering.

Key decisions

01 — Prioritizing use cases

We prioritized the states of users’ keys: no keys registered, with or without a portability request, registered keys, or keys with portability in progress. Refund requests under MED, the Central Bank’s refund mechanism for Pix fraud, take priority over key registration.

02 — Different flows for each audience

Individuals and sellers share the same flow, but they come for different reasons. Individuals are invited to use their CPF and phone as keys to pay faster, and sellers their CNPJ and phone to start receiving. With the UX writer, I designed a first-time use (FTU) modal for each profile, with and without registered keys, and I created the four illustrations that carry each message.

As the team’s go-to person for illustration and visual design, I often drew and designed flows in the same project. I see illustration as context for the flow, making clearer what we’re asking of people and giving the experience a more human tone.

03 — Display rules

A user can have several refund requests and portabilities open at once, and stacking every alert would bury what matters. We shipped a minimum version first and defined the full display rules as the next step, with one message at a time, prioritized by status.

Outcome

The Pix area shipped on web on the planned deadline, and the team later expanded it with Pix Copia e Cola. I no longer have access to post-launch data.

What I’d do differently

Keep the post-launch data. I lost access to it, so I can’t show the real impact this flow had.
