Summary

I designed the consent and data-sharing journeys for Open Finance at Mercado Pago, among the first in Brazil under the new Central Bank rules: how people bring their data from other banks into the app, and how they share Mercado Pago data with other institutions.

Context & challenge

Open Finance asks people to share sensitive financial data between institutions. The Central Bank defined strict rules for every step, and users had no mental model for the concept. The flow had to be compliant, transparent and simple enough that people wouldn’t abandon it halfway.

Process

01

Regulation into constraints

Mapped what the Central Bank made mandatory and what was open to design.

02

Explore and test

Explored variations of the authorization steps and tested them in in-person usability sessions.

03

Plain language

Rewrote the consent copy from legal language into plain language, without losing compliance.

04

Review before handoff

Reviewed every step with legal/compliance and engineering before handoff.

Key decisions

01 — Showing where you are in the consent

Before any action, users see what data is shared, with which institution and for how long. After launch, metrics showed people abandoning the flow halfway through, so I added a stepper: users always know which step they are on and how much is left to finish.

02 — Putting the most-used banks first

Choosing where to bring data from is the first step of the flow, and a long list of institutions slowed people down. I looked into which banks users connected most and checked feasibility with engineering: we could surface the most-used banks at the top as shortcuts, with search and the full list right below.

03 — Consent you can see and take back

Shared data lives in an easy-to-find menu. We invested in a clear, visual interface so users can track active consents and revoke them at any time.

Outcome

The journeys launched with Brazil’s first wave of Open Finance and won 4 Open Summit Awards in 2022: Case of the Year, Payment Initiation, Financial Inclusion and Best B2C Case. After launch, I kept iterating on drop-off data, which led to the stepper.

What I learned

Regulation defines what must be shown, but design decides whether people understand it.
