Summary

I joined the Open Finance team at Mercado Pago, Mercado Livre’s fintech, to design the consent and data-sharing journeys, among the first in Brazil under the new Central Bank rules: how people bring their data from other banks into the app, and how they share Mercado Pago data with other institutions.

I designed the data-in and data-out journeys together with the design team, alongside product, engineering and legal.

Context & challenge

Open Finance asks people to share sensitive financial data between institutions. The concept was new in Brazil and globally: a Mercado Pago survey found that 44% of Brazilians didn’t know what Open Finance was. On top of that, the Central Bank defined strict rules for every step. The flow had to be compliant, transparent and simple enough that people wouldn’t abandon it halfway.

Key decisions

01 — Showing where you are in the consent

Before any action, users see what data is shared, with which institution and for how long. After launch, metrics showed people abandoning the flow halfway through, so I designed a stepper: users always know which step they are on and how much is left to finish.

02 — Putting the most-used banks first

Choosing where to bring data from is the first step of the flow, and a long list of institutions slowed people down. We looked into which banks users connected most and checked feasibility with engineering, then I designed the most-used banks as shortcuts at the top, with search and the full list right below.

03 — Consent you can see and take back

Shared data lives in an easy-to-find menu. We invested in a clear, visual interface so users can track active consents, see when they expire and revoke them at any time.

Outcome

The journeys launched with Brazil’s first wave of Open Finance and were recognized in 4 categories at the Open Summit Awards 2022, including 2nd place for Case of the Year. By early 2023, around 1 million Mercado Pago clients had shared their data through Open Finance (company figures reported in the press).

What I learned

This was my first project at a company the size of Mercado Livre, with a brand-new technology and a context strictly shaped by regulation. It deepened my knowledge across the whole UX process and information architecture.

Regulation defines what must be shown. The work was translating new, and sometimes really dry, information into something people could actually understand.
