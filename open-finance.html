Summary

I joined the Open Finance team at Mercado Pago, Mercado Livre’s fintech, to design the consent and data-sharing journeys, among the first in Brazil under the new Central Bank rules.

I designed the data-in and data-out journeys together with the design team, alongside product, engineering and legal.

Context & challenge

Open Finance lets people share financial data between institutions. The concept was new in Brazil and globally: a Mercado Pago survey found that 44% of Brazilians didn’t know what Open Finance was. On top of that, the Central Bank defined strict rules for every step. The flow had to be compliant, transparent and simple enough that people wouldn’t abandon it halfway.

Key decisions

01 — Showing where you are in the consent

Before any action, users see what data is shared, with which institution and for how long. After launch, the metrics showed people dropping out halfway through the flow, so we designed the stepper. To keep people in the flow, the component makes clear which step they’re on.

02 — Putting the most-used banks first

Choosing where to bring data from is the first step of the flow. At launch, it started with search and an alphabetical list of institutions. Once we had data on which banks people connected most, we checked with engineering whether a redesign of the list was feasible. As an iteration, I designed a shortcut with the top 5 banks at the top, with search and the full list right below.

03 — Visibility and control over consents

Shared data lives in an easy-to-find menu. Each consent shows its status at a glance (active, expiring, expired or ended), so users can track, renew or revoke it at any time.

Outcome

The journeys launched with Brazil’s first wave of Open Finance and were recognized in 4 categories at the Open Summit Awards 2022, including 2nd place for Case of the Year. By early 2023, around 1 million Mercado Pago clients had shared their data through Open Finance (company figures reported in the press).

What I learned

Coming from Guiabolso, where the room to imagine felt endless and we were still creating and refining our ways of working, this was my first time designing inside a large company, within banking regulation. With more established processes and a defined scope, creativity moved somewhere else, making room for more critical thinking about what was real and feasible in product design.
