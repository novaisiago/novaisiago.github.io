Summary

In 2023, Brazil’s Central Bank required every payment app to let users set Pix limits and manage trusted contacts. At Mercado Pago, I designed the flows for My Limits and Safe Contacts, balancing security with the friction of everyday payments.

After delivering and iterating on the Open Finance flows, the company’s priorities moved to Pix, the area with the most money moving through the app. That year I joined the Pix squad and, with the team on this project, designed the navigation architecture, flows and handoff for My Limits, limits by period and Safe Contacts, for individual and business accounts. I also designed the new limit cards and their edge cases in feedback screens and notifications.

Context & challenge

Pix is Brazil’s instant payment system, regulated by the Central Bank. Version 6.4 of the Pix Experience Manual required features the app didn’t have: trusted contacts, day and night limits, and limit-increase requests for Pix Saque (cash withdrawal at stores) and Pix Troco (cash back on purchases).

With the Central Bank’s manual as the guide to which features were required, and by when, I explored ways to deliver a functional, low-friction experience in the menu structure, in how limits are explained, and in error states and notifications.

Key decisions

01 — Ordering the menu around a real fear

The card sorting was framed around a familiar fear: losing your phone or being robbed at night. Transfers came first, as the most frequent action, and Safe Contacts came last. The final order is Transfers → Withdrawals → Transaction period → Safe Contacts: limits come first, and the exception to the rules comes last.

In the wording test led by our UX writer, “R$ 1,000 for the period” read more clearly than “R$ 1,000 in total”. Next to “remaining”, the word “total” made it unclear whether the amount was the full limit or what was left.

02 — Planning for every possible path

I mapped and designed every state before the screens, from setting a limit to R$ 0 (which blocks transfers) to exceeding the R$ 50k cap. Increases take up to 24 hours while decreases are immediate, so the confirmation screen and the push notification explain the wait, with an illustration that reinforces the context.

03 — Optimizing development

Safe Contacts reuses components from Pix key registration, cutting development effort under a fixed deadline and keeping patterns consistent across the Pix area. The only new components were the day/night limit cards and their variations, which I refined over several feedback rounds and documented with engineering.

Outcome

We shipped on the regulatory deadline, and the work was validated by the leadership team for payments and money rails. Impact data is confidential.

What I learned

A regulatory deadline sometimes means doing the best you can with the time and resources you have. It takes flexibility, or “jogo de cintura”, as we say in Portuguese. The flow held up well after launch, and it was also one of my most end-to-end projects: from the release of the manual to going live and following the data, bringing weekly updates to the team and working through feedback.
