Summary

In 2023, Brazil’s Central Bank required every payment app to let users set Pix limits and manage trusted contacts. At Mercado Pago, I designed My Limits and Safe Contacts, balancing protection against theft with everyday payment friction. We shipped every mandatory feature by the July 3 deadline.

As a product designer on the Pix squad, working with a UX writer and a senior designer, I designed the navigation architecture, flows and handoff for My Limits, limits by period and Safe Contacts, for individual and business accounts, plus the new limit cards, feedback screens and notifications.

Context & challenge

Pix is Brazil’s instant payment system, regulated by the Central Bank. Version 6.4 of the Pix Experience Manual required features the app didn’t have: trusted contacts, day and night limits, and limit-increase requests for Pix Saque (cash withdrawal at stores) and Pix Troco (cash back on purchases). All of it had to fit into a Pix experience used by millions, within one quarter.

The law fixed the features and the deadline. The menu structure, how limits are explained, error states and notifications were open to design, and that is where I spent my effort.

02

Mapped every use case in the manual, plus errors and edge cases, before drawing screens.

03

Ran a card sorting and a wording test with the UX writer (8 internal participants, treated as directional).

04

Review before handoff

Feedback rounds with domain specialists, design system and engineering.

Key decisions

01 — Ordering the menu around a real fear

We framed the card sorting around losing your phone or being robbed at night. Among 8 internal colleagues, 7 put Transfers first and 5 put Safe Contacts last. The final order is Transfers → Withdrawals → Transaction period → Safe Contacts: limits come first, and the exception to the rules comes last.

All 8 also chose “R$ 1,000 for the period” over “R$ 1,000 in total”. Next to “remaining”, the word “total” made it unclear whether the amount was the full limit or what was left.

02 — Designing for failure, not just the happy path

I designed every state before the screens, from setting a limit to R$ 0 (which blocks transfers) to exceeding the R$ 50k cap. Increases take up to 24 hours while decreases are immediate, so the confirmation and the push notification explain the delay. Users never have to wonder whether a change worked.

03 — Reusing what users already know

Safe Contacts reuses the bottom sheet and the CPF / CNPJ / phone tag from Pix key registration, already familiar to users and to engineering. The only new components were the day/night limit cards and their variations, documented with engineering.

Whether someone adds a contact by CPF, CNPJ, phone, e-mail, random key or a past transfer, the flow uses one unified input and the same error states: key not found, attempt limit reached, own account, already registered.

Outcome

We shipped on the regulatory deadline, and the work was validated by the leadership team for payments and money rails. Impact data is confidential, but I’m happy to walk through the decisions in an interview.

What I learned

Designing under a regulatory deadline taught me to separate what is fixed by law from what is open to design, and to spend testing time only on the second.
